 STRICT FRONTEND & UX REQUIREMENTS

This addendum is mandatory. It supplements the main application requirements and takes priority over any default frontend design decisions.

The objective is to deliver a premium automotive e-commerce experience that feels professionally designed, commercially credible, and carefully engineered—not a generic AI-generated storefront.

1. Establish a Distinct Visual Identity

Design the entire customer-facing application around the identity of RANG ATV/AUTO PART/V ACCESSORIES.

Before implementing the interface, establish a cohesive visual system covering:

* Typography and font hierarchy.
* Brand colors and neutral surfaces.
* Spacing and layout proportions.
* Border treatments, shadows, and radii.
* Iconography and interaction states.
* Product image presentation.
* Buttons, forms, cards, and navigation.

The design should communicate automotive expertise, reliability, and confidence.

Avoid generic blue-and-white SaaS styling, oversized gradient backgrounds, excessive rounded cards, unnecessary decorative elements, and template-like layouts.

Use a restrained, premium visual language appropriate for an established automotive parts retailer.

2. Build a Real E-Commerce Interface, Not a Landing Page

The primary experience is product discovery and purchasing.

The homepage must be designed around the customer’s shopping journey rather than simply presenting a collection of promotional sections.

Implement a carefully structured storefront with:

* A professional header and shopping navigation.
* A prominent, functional product search.
* Clearly organized automotive product categories.
* A purposeful promotional or featured-product area.
* Product collections that help customers discover relevant items.
* A visually clear product catalog.
* Consistent product cards with useful information.
* A persistent, accessible shopping cart entry point.

The homepage must feel complete without becoming unnecessarily long or cluttered.

Every section must serve a clear commercial or navigational purpose.

3. Automotive Product Discovery

Automotive parts shopping has different requirements from ordinary retail.

Customers may know the exact part they need, or they may only know their vehicle.

Design the product discovery experience around both situations.

Implement:

* Fast product search with useful suggestions.
* Search by product name, SKU, brand, or relevant specifications.
* Category-based browsing.
* Useful filters for price, brand, availability, and other relevant attributes.
* Sorting by price, relevance, and newest products.
* A vehicle compatibility selector where appropriate.

The vehicle selector should allow customers to identify their vehicle using relevant information such as make, model, and year.

When compatibility information is available, clearly communicate whether a product fits the selected vehicle.

Never imply compatibility when the information has not been verified.

Search, filtering, and category navigation must work with actual backend data.

Do not implement decorative search bars or filters that do nothing.

4. Product Cards and Product Detail Experience

Product presentation must be designed specifically for automotive parts and accessories.

Product cards should prioritize:

* Clear product imagery.
* Product name.
* Brand or manufacturer where available.
* Price and applicable sale price.
* Stock availability.
* Relevant compatibility information.
* A clear action for viewing or purchasing the product.

Avoid excessive information that makes the catalog difficult to scan.

The product detail page must provide a strong purchasing experience.

Include:

* A high-quality image gallery with zoom or full-image viewing.
* Product name, brand, SKU, and price.
* Stock availability.
* Specifications and product description.
* Vehicle compatibility information where applicable.
* Quantity selection.
* Add-to-cart functionality.
* Delivery or collection information where available.
* Relevant related products.

The purchase action should be visually prominent without overwhelming the page.

Ensure that the layout remains practical for products with long names, multiple specifications, or several images.

5. Modern Interaction Design

Use modern frontend interaction patterns that improve usability without adding unnecessary complexity.

Implement:

* Immediate visual feedback for cart actions.
* Loading states for asynchronous operations.
* Skeleton loading where appropriate.
* Clear empty states.
* Helpful validation messages.
* Success and error notifications.
* Optimistic updates only where safe.
* Smooth but restrained transitions.
* Proper disabled states for unavailable actions.

Avoid full-page reloads for ordinary shopping interactions.

Do not use blocking browser alerts for routine application feedback.

Ensure that interactions remain predictable and that the interface never appears frozen during network operations.

6. Mobile Shopping Experience

The mobile interface must receive the same design attention as the desktop interface.

Design for customers shopping on smartphones, including users with limited screen space or slower internet connections.

Prioritize:

* Compact and intuitive navigation.
* Accessible product search.
* Easy-to-use category browsing.
* Readable product information.
* Comfortable touch targets.
* Clear cart and checkout actions.
* Mobile-friendly forms.
* Efficient image loading.

Use a mobile navigation pattern appropriate for the application.

If a bottom navigation bar is introduced, ensure that it serves a clear purpose and does not compete with the primary shopping actions.

The mobile checkout must be particularly simple and reliable.

Do not merely shrink the desktop layout.

7. Checkout UX

The checkout experience must minimize unnecessary friction.

Use a clear, well-structured checkout flow that collects only the information required to complete an order.

Customers must always understand:

* What they are purchasing.
* How much they are paying.
* What delivery or collection options are available.
* Which payment method they have selected.
* Whether their order has been successfully submitted.

Keep the order summary accessible throughout checkout.

Preserve cart contents when customers navigate away or refresh the page.

Clearly distinguish between an order being submitted, confirmed, paid, and fulfilled.

Prevent accidental duplicate submissions and provide helpful feedback when an operation fails.

8. Professional Administrative Interface

The administrative dashboard is part of the product and must not be treated as an afterthought.

Design a professional operational interface for staff managing products, stock, and orders.

Use:

* Clear navigation.
* Well-organized data tables.
* Useful search and filtering.
* Efficient product editing.
* Clear order status indicators.
* Contextual actions.
* Confirmation for destructive operations.
* Meaningful dashboard metrics.
* Responsive layouts for smaller screens.

Prioritize information density and operational efficiency without making the dashboard visually overwhelming.

The interface should help staff complete routine business tasks quickly.

9. Frontend Engineering Standards

Use modern, maintainable frontend practices.

The application must include:

* Reusable design-system components.
* Consistent form handling and validation.
* Clear separation between UI components and business logic.
* Proper loading, error, empty, and success states.
* Accessible semantic HTML.
* Keyboard navigation.
* Appropriate focus management.
* Responsive image handling.
* Efficient rendering and asset loading.

Use established libraries where they genuinely improve reliability or maintainability.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies and do not introduce complexity simply to appear modern.

The frontend must consume real backend functionality and remain consistent with the application’s actual data.

Do not use hardcoded product data, simulated checkout behavior, or placeholder interactions as substitutes for implemented features.

10. Visual Quality Verification

Before considering the frontend complete, inspect the actual application at desktop and mobile sizes.

Evaluate the following:

* Does the interface look like a professional automotive retailer?
* Is the product catalog visually compelling and easy to navigate?
* Can customers quickly find a specific part?
* Are product images presented consistently?
* Is the shopping cart easy to access?
* Is checkout clear and frictionless?
* Does the mobile interface feel deliberately designed?
* Are all interactive elements functional?
* Are there any awkward layouts, excessive whitespace, inconsistent spacing, or broken responsive states?

Fix visual and usability problems discovered during this review.

Do not declare the frontend complete simply because the pages render without errors.

Final requirement: Deliver a polished, commercially credible automotive e-commerce experience with the visual quality, interaction design, and usability expected from a professionally developed online retail platform. Every design decision must support the actual shopping and business-management workflows.
