 RANG ATV/AUTO PART/V ACCESSORIES

Production-Ready Automotive E-Commerce Platform

You are a senior full-stack software engineer, product designer, database architect, and quality assurance engineer responsible for delivering a complete, production-ready e-commerce application for RANG ATV/AUTO PART/V ACCESSORIES.

Your responsibility is not simply to generate a website that looks good. You must build a complete, functional, secure, maintainable, and commercially usable application that the business can actually operate.

Understand the business, make appropriate technical decisions, implement the complete system, and verify that it works.

Do not deliver a toy application, incomplete MVP, static frontend, or a collection of disconnected features.

1. Business and Product Vision

RANG ATV/AUTO PART/V ACCESSORIES is an automotive parts and accessories business.

The application must provide a professional online shopping experience where customers can discover automotive products, understand their specifications, find compatible parts, and purchase products.

The business must also have a secure administrative system for managing its products, inventory, orders, customers, and general operations.

Build a complete automotive e-commerce platform with two integrated experiences:

1. A premium customer-facing online store.
2. A secure business management dashboard.

Both experiences must use the same backend and database.

The final application must be suitable for a real business that intends to grow its product catalog, customer base, and sales.

2. Technical Architecture

Use a simple, maintainable, and scalable three-layer architecture:

* Frontend
* Backend
* PostgreSQL database

You have the freedom to select the most appropriate technologies, frameworks, libraries, and supporting services.

Choose a stack that is reliable, widely supported, maintainable, and appropriate for a production e-commerce application.

Do not introduce unnecessary infrastructure, microservices, or architectural complexity.

The application should be easy to develop, deploy, maintain, and transfer between hosting providers.

The frontend, backend, and database must be properly separated while remaining part of one coherent application.

Use environment variables for configuration and secrets.

The application must not depend on the development environment to function after deployment.

3. Frontend and User Experience

The frontend is a critical part of this project.

Do not sacrifice the quality of the user interface or user experience in favor of backend implementation.

Design a premium, modern, professional automotive e-commerce experience.

The visual identity should communicate:

* Reliability
* Quality
* Professionalism
* Trust
* Automotive expertise

Use a cohesive design system with carefully selected typography, colors, spacing, components, icons, and interactions.

Avoid generic templates, childish designs, excessive gradients, unnecessary animations, and visually overwhelming layouts.

The interface must feel like a legitimate automotive business, not a generic AI-generated storefront.

Customer-facing pages

Implement the complete customer experience, including:

* Home page
* Product catalog
* Product category pages
* Product search
* Product filtering and sorting
* Product detail pages
* Shopping cart
* Checkout
* Order confirmation
* Customer registration and login
* Customer account dashboard
* Order history and order tracking
* Contact page
* About page
* Privacy policy and terms of service

The home page should include a professionally designed hero section, product categories, featured products, promotional sections, and clear shopping navigation.

Product cards must display relevant information such as product images, names, prices, availability, and other useful details.

Product detail pages must support multiple images, detailed descriptions, specifications, pricing, stock availability, and vehicle compatibility where applicable.

Mobile-first experience

The website must be fully responsive and optimized for mobile devices.

Customers should be able to browse products, add items to their cart, and complete their orders comfortably on a mobile phone.

Pay particular attention to navigation, product cards, search, filters, forms, and checkout.

The mobile experience must not be a reduced version of the desktop experience.

It must be deliberately designed for mobile usability.

4. Automotive Product Management

Build the product system around the actual needs of an automotive parts business.

Products should support:

* Product name
* SKU or unique product identifier
* Category
* Brand
* Price
* Sale price where applicable
* Description
* Product specifications
* Product images
* Stock quantity
* Availability status
* Vehicle compatibility information where applicable

The product catalog should support categories such as:

* ATV parts and accessories
* Car parts
* Motorcycle parts where applicable
* Vehicle accessories
* Other automotive products

The business must be able to create, edit, organize, publish, unpublish, and delete products through the administrative dashboard.

The system must support searching, filtering, and organizing a growing product catalog without requiring code changes.

Implement a sensible vehicle compatibility structure where products can be associated with relevant vehicle makes, models, years, and specifications.

Do not assume that every automotive product has vehicle compatibility information.

5. Shopping Cart and Checkout

Implement a complete and functional shopping and ordering process.

Customers must be able to:

* Add products to their cart.
* Change product quantities.
* Remove products.
* Review their order before checkout.
* Enter their contact and delivery information.
* Select an available payment method.
* Submit their order.
* Receive an order confirmation.

The backend must validate product availability, quantities, prices, and order totals.

Never trust prices, stock quantities, or payment status submitted by the frontend.

The server must calculate the final order total using authoritative database values.

Prevent customers from purchasing quantities that exceed available inventory.

Ensure that stock is handled correctly when orders are created, cancelled, or fulfilled.

The checkout process must be reliable and protect against duplicate order submissions.

6. Payments and Delivery

Design the payment system so that the business can support appropriate payment methods without requiring a complete architectural rewrite.

Support the appropriate payment options for the business, including cash on delivery and online payment integration where available.

If an online payment provider is selected, implement its integration using official documentation and secure server-side verification.

Do not mark an order as paid merely because the frontend reports a successful payment.

The system must distinguish between:

* Pending payment
* Paid
* Failed
* Refunded
* Cash on delivery

Payment methods must be configurable according to the business’s actual operating requirements.

Delivery information should support customer addresses, delivery fees, delivery methods, and order fulfillment status.

Provide a clear way for administrators to manage delivery and fulfillment.

Do not invent payment provider credentials or pretend an integration is operational without valid configuration and successful testing.

7. Administrative Dashboard

Build a professional, secure administrative dashboard.

The dashboard must allow authorized staff to manage the business without directly interacting with the database.

Include:

* Overview of sales and orders
* Product management
* Category management
* Inventory management
* Order management
* Customer management
* Payment status management
* Delivery and fulfillment management
* Basic sales reports
* Store settings

The dashboard must display useful business information without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

Administrators should be able to search, filter, and manage records efficiently.

Implement appropriate authorization so that customers cannot access administrative functionality.

All important administrative operations must be validated on the backend.

8. Backend and Database

Build a reliable backend with a properly structured PostgreSQL database.

Use an appropriate database access layer and migrations.

The database should include the necessary entities for:

* Users
* Roles and permissions
* Products
* Categories
* Product images
* Vehicle compatibility
* Shopping carts
* Orders
* Order items
* Payments
* Delivery information
* Store settings

Use appropriate relationships, constraints, indexes, and validation.

Design the database to support future growth without unnecessary complexity.

Implement proper error handling, logging, and consistent API responses.

Avoid duplicating business logic across the frontend and backend.

Important operations such as order creation, stock updates, payment verification, and order cancellation must be handled safely and consistently.

9. Database-Portable File Storage

A major requirement is that the application must remain portable when moved between hosting platforms or development environments.

The PostgreSQL database must be the authoritative source of application data.

Do not make the application dependent on files stored only inside the development environment or a temporary hosting filesystem.

For product images and other application files, implement a storage strategy that supports portability.

Prefer storing file content in PostgreSQL using an appropriate binary storage mechanism, such as BYTEA, when practical.

Do not store large image files as ordinary Base64 strings in database text columns without a compelling reason.

If Base64 encoding is required for compatibility, implement it carefully and avoid unnecessary encoding and decoding overhead.

The application must support:

* Uploading product images
* Validating file types and sizes
* Secure file access
* Storing file metadata
* Retrieving images reliably
* Exporting stored files
* Restoring files from a database backup

If an external storage provider is used, make it replaceable through a storage abstraction rather than tightly coupling the application to one provider.

The business must not lose its product images or other important files when moving away from a particular development or hosting platform.

10. Security and Reliability

Implement production-appropriate security throughout the application.

Include:

* Secure authentication
* Proper password hashing
* Server-side authorization
* Input validation
* Protection against common web vulnerabilities
* Secure handling of environment variables
* Safe database queries
* Rate limiting where appropriate
* Secure file upload validation
* Appropriate session management
* Protection against unauthorized administrative access

Do not expose sensitive information through API responses, frontend code, logs, or error messages.

Implement proper error handling so that users receive helpful messages without exposing internal system details.

The application must not rely on frontend-only security checks.

11. Scalability and Maintainability

Build the application so that it can grow with the business.

Use clear separation of concerns, reusable components, maintainable code, and sensible project organization.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies and complicated infrastructure.

The system should support a growing catalog, increasing customer traffic, and additional features without requiring a complete rewrite.

Implement pagination for large datasets and use appropriate database indexes.

Optimize images and frontend assets.

Do not introduce expensive or complex infrastructure unless it is genuinely necessary.

12. Production Readiness

The application must be completed and verified before delivery.

Do not stop when the frontend looks finished.

Do not stop when the database schema has been created.

Do not stop when the main features have been implemented.

Complete the entire application and verify that its important workflows function correctly.

Test at least the following:

1. Customer registration and login.
2. Product browsing and searching.
3. Product detail pages.
4. Shopping cart operations.
5. Checkout and order creation.
6. Inventory updates.
7. Payment status handling.
8. Order management.
9. Administrative authentication and authorization.
10. Product creation, editing, and deletion.
11. Product image uploads and retrieval.
12. Mobile responsiveness.
13. Database persistence.
14. Application behavior after restarting the server.

Test both successful and unsuccessful scenarios.

Fix the issues discovered during testing.

Do not claim that the application is production-ready if important functionality is incomplete or untested.

13. Deployment and Portability

Prepare the application for deployment to a real production environment.

Provide:

* A clear project structure
* Database migrations
* Environment variable documentation
* Production configuration
* A deployment guide
* Database backup and restoration instructions
* A way to export and restore application data
* Instructions for setting up the application outside the original development environment

The application must be capable of running independently on a compatible hosting platform with a PostgreSQL database.

Do not rely on temporary development URLs, development-only storage, or undocumented platform-specific behavior.

14. Execution Instructions

You are responsible for making the technical decisions required to achieve this goal.

Do not ask me to choose a framework, database library, folder structure, or implementation detail unless a decision genuinely requires my input.

Make reasonable assumptions where information is missing.

Prioritize a complete, coherent, functional application over unnecessary technical complexity.

Before implementation, inspect the existing project and determine what is already available.

If the project is empty, establish an appropriate production-ready structure.

If existing functionality is present, preserve and improve it rather than unnecessarily rebuilding everything.

Implement the application in logical stages, but do not treat the completion of an individual stage as completion of the project.

Maintain a clear list of outstanding requirements and resolve them before declaring the work complete.

If a feature depends on credentials, external services, or information that is not available, implement the appropriate integration structure, clearly identify the missing configuration, and provide instructions for completing it.

Do not simulate real functionality with fake data in production-facing workflows.

Use realistic seed data only for development and testing.

15. Final Delivery Requirements

At the end of the project, provide:

* A complete, functional application.
* A professional customer-facing storefront.
* A secure administrative dashboard.
* A properly structured PostgreSQL database.
* Working product, cart, checkout, and order management functionality.
* A responsive, polished frontend.
* A maintainable backend.
* Database migrations.
* Deployment documentation.
* Backup and restoration instructions.
* A summary of completed features.
* A list of any remaining limitations or external configuration requirements.
* Results of the important tests performed.

The final result must be a complete business application that RANG ATV/AUTO PART/V ACCESSORIES can use to operate its online store.

This addendum is mandatory. It supplements the main application requirements and takes priority over any default frontend design decisions.

The objective is to deliver a premium automotive e-commerce experience that feels professionally designed, commercially credible, and carefully engineered—not a generic AI-generated storefront.

1. Establish a Distinct Visual Identity

Design the entire customer-facing application around the identity of RANG ATV/AUTO PART/V ACCESSORIES.

Before implementing the interface, establish a cohesive visual system covering:

* Typography and font hierarchy.
* Brand colors and neutral surfaces.
* Spacing and layout proportions.
* Border treatments, shadows, and radii.
* Iconography and interaction states.
* Product image presentation.
* Buttons, forms, cards, and navigation.

The design should communicate automotive expertise, reliability, and confidence.

Avoid generic blue-and-white SaaS styling, oversized gradient backgrounds, excessive rounded cards, unnecessary decorative elements, and template-like layouts.

Use a restrained, premium visual language appropriate for an established automotive parts retailer.

2. Build a Real E-Commerce Interface, Not a Landing Page

The primary experience is product discovery and purchasing.

The homepage must be designed around the customer’s shopping journey rather than simply presenting a collection of promotional sections.

Implement a carefully structured storefront with:

* A professional header and shopping navigation.
* A prominent, functional product search.
* Clearly organized automotive product categories.
* A purposeful promotional or featured-product area.
* Product collections that help customers discover relevant items.
* A visually clear product catalog.
* Consistent product cards with useful information.
* A persistent, accessible shopping cart entry point.

The homepage must feel complete without becoming unnecessarily long or cluttered.

Every section must serve a clear commercial or navigational purpose.

3. Automotive Product Discovery

Automotive parts shopping has different requirements from ordinary retail.

Customers may know the exact part they need, or they may only know their vehicle.

Design the product discovery experience around both situations.

Implement:

* Fast product search with useful suggestions.
* Search by product name, SKU, brand, or relevant specifications.
* Category-based browsing.
* Useful filters for price, brand, availability, and other relevant attributes.
* Sorting by price, relevance, and newest products.
* A vehicle compatibility selector where appropriate.

The vehicle selector should allow customers to identify their vehicle using relevant information such as make, model, and year.

When compatibility information is available, clearly communicate whether a product fits the selected vehicle.

Never imply compatibility when the information has not been verified.

Search, filtering, and category navigation must work with actual backend data.

Do not implement decorative search bars or filters that do nothing.

4. Product Cards and Product Detail Experience

Product presentation must be designed specifically for automotive parts and accessories.

Product cards should prioritize:

* Clear product imagery.
* Product name.
* Brand or manufacturer where available.
* Price and applicable sale price.
* Stock availability.
* Relevant compatibility information.
* A clear action for viewing or purchasing the product.

Avoid excessive information that makes the catalog difficult to scan.

The product detail page must provide a strong purchasing experience.

Include:

* A high-quality image gallery with zoom or full-image viewing.
* Product name, brand, SKU, and price.
* Stock availability.
* Specifications and product description.
* Vehicle compatibility information where applicable.
* Quantity selection.
* Add-to-cart functionality.
* Delivery or collection information where available.
* Relevant related products.

The purchase action should be visually prominent without overwhelming the page.

Ensure that the layout remains practical for products with long names, multiple specifications, or several images.

5. Modern Interaction Design

Use modern frontend interaction patterns that improve usability without adding unnecessary complexity.

Implement:

* Immediate visual feedback for cart actions.
* Loading states for asynchronous operations.
* Skeleton loading where appropriate.
* Clear empty states.
* Helpful validation messages.
* Success and error notifications.
* Optimistic updates only where safe.
* Smooth but restrained transitions.
* Proper disabled states for unavailable actions.

Avoid full-page reloads for ordinary shopping interactions.

Do not use blocking browser alerts for routine application feedback.

Ensure that interactions remain predictable and that the interface never appears frozen during network operations.

6. Mobile Shopping Experience

The mobile interface must receive the same design attention as the desktop interface.

Design for customers shopping on smartphones, including users with limited screen space or slower internet connections.

Prioritize:

* Compact and intuitive navigation.
* Accessible product search.
* Easy-to-use category browsing.
* Readable product information.
* Comfortable touch targets.
* Clear cart and checkout actions.
* Mobile-friendly forms.
* Efficient image loading.

Use a mobile navigation pattern appropriate for the application.

If a bottom navigation bar is introduced, ensure that it serves a clear purpose and does not compete with the primary shopping actions.

The mobile checkout must be particularly simple and reliable.

Do not merely shrink the desktop layout.

7. Checkout UX

The checkout experience must minimize unnecessary friction.

Use a clear, well-structured checkout flow that collects only the information required to complete an order.

Customers must always understand:

* What they are purchasing.
* How much they are paying.
* What delivery or collection options are available.
* Which payment method they have selected.
* Whether their order has been successfully submitted.

Keep the order summary accessible throughout checkout.

Preserve cart contents when customers navigate away or refresh the page.

Clearly distinguish between an order being submitted, confirmed, paid, and fulfilled.

Prevent accidental duplicate submissions and provide helpful feedback when an operation fails.

8. Professional Administrative Interface

The administrative dashboard is part of the product and must not be treated as an afterthought.

Design a professional operational interface for staff managing products, stock, and orders.

Use:

* Clear navigation.
* Well-organized data tables.
* Useful search and filtering.
* Efficient product editing.
* Clear order status indicators.
* Contextual actions.
* Confirmation for destructive operations.
* Meaningful dashboard metrics.
* Responsive layouts for smaller screens.

Prioritize information density and operational efficiency without making the dashboard visually overwhelming.

The interface should help staff complete routine business tasks quickly.

9. Frontend Engineering Standards

Use modern, maintainable frontend practices.

The application must include:

* Reusable design-system components.
* Consistent form handling and validation.
* Clear separation between UI components and business logic.
* Proper loading, error, empty, and success states.
* Accessible semantic HTML.
* Keyboard navigation.
* Appropriate focus management.
* Responsive image handling.
* Efficient rendering and asset loading.

Use established libraries where they genuinely improve reliability or maintainability.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies and do not introduce complexity simply to appear modern.

The frontend must consume real backend functionality and remain consistent with the application’s actual data.

Do not use hardcoded product data, simulated checkout behavior, or placeholder interactions as substitutes for implemented features.

10. Visual Quality Verification

Before considering the frontend complete, inspect the actual application at desktop and mobile sizes.

Evaluate the following:

* Does the interface look like a professional automotive retailer?
* Is the product catalog visually compelling and easy to navigate?
* Can customers quickly find a specific part?
* Are product images presented consistently?
* Is the shopping cart easy to access?
* Is checkout clear and frictionless?
* Does the mobile interface feel deliberately designed?
* Are all interactive elements functional?
* Are there any awkward layouts, excessive whitespace, inconsistent spacing, or broken responsive states?

Fix visual and usability problems discovered during this review.

Do not declare the frontend complete simply because the pages render without errors.

Final requirement: Deliver a polished, commercially credible automotive e-commerce experience with the visual quality, interaction design, and usability expected from a professionally developed online retail platform. Every design decision must support the actual shopping and business-management workflows.
